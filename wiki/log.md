* **Create**: [One runtime call serves div and mod, and the call cannot tell you which](/observations/one-helper-two-operators/observation.md). Borland compiles LongInt `div` and LongInt `mod` to the SAME runtime routine, whose tail is the whole rule -- MOV ECX,EDX then SHLD EDX,EAX,16 and SHLD EBX,ECX,16, so the quotient comes back in DX:AX and the remainder in CX:BX. The quotient is already where the next expression expects a value, so `div` needs no fixup; `mod` has to move the other pair across. The operator is therefore not in the call at all, it is in whether MOV AX,CX / MOV DX,BX follows -- and its ABSENCE is the half that gets missed. Expensive because both spellings compile and run: no error, no warning, just a number of the wrong magnitude flowing into whatever the expression feeds, and two instructions' difference in a routine hundreds of bytes long. And it repeats, because such an expression is written once and copied. Carries the free second reading, which is the transferable part: check the value's RANGE against what consumes it. Here V fed a DAC red channel valid 0..63; `div` gave 300..480 and `mod` gave 0 or 60. Register evidence and range agreed and either alone would have sufficed. Blind spot: a pair of moves after a call is not unique to `mod` -- what identifies it is moving FROM the pair that particular helper leaves the remainder in, so confirm the helper first. Written after a guess-by-analogy on the same spans measured nothing and was reverted; reading the helper took two minutes and settled it.
